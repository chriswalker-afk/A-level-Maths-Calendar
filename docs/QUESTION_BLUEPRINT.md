# A Level Maths Calendar — Detailed Question Blueprint

> **Version 1 with Pass 1 revision · 4 November 2026 · Pearson Edexcel A Level Mathematics (9MA0)**
> This is the **authoritative content/authoring blueprint** for the first programme. It records the agreed design. It is not a claim that all questions have already been written.
> Programme 1 prepares for **internal exams: all Pure + Year 1 Statistics & Mechanics**. Programme 2 will extend from **1 February to 16 June 2027** to **the full 9MA0 syllabus**, with both years of Applied.
> Teacher-set Stage 1 dates are **not yet specified**. Do not assume a date for the first ten-week programme; the site starts with a configurable **manual release count**.

## Pedagogical intent

A deliberately more challenging alternative to the existing 29-week *Fluency & Recall* support programme. Students here can already perform many techniques; they need practice **choosing the mathematical method, combining previously separate ideas, constructing and communicating proofs, and interpreting models**.

- **AO1:** Use standard techniques and recall mathematical facts (practice target ~25%).
- **AO2:** Reason, interpret, communicate, construct arguments and proof (~40% practice target).
- **AO3:** Solve problems, construct/use mathematical models and evaluate conclusions (~35% practice target).
- These are **resource-authoring targets** (AO attribution by marks in parts), **not Pearson's exam-wide weightings**. Full mocks should more closely resemble actual papers.
- Genuine cross-topic challenges; avoid cosmetic contexts or contrived multi-part questions.
- Strict total per week **4h essential / 5h recommended / 6h optional**, including self-marking, correction and reflection.

Question design gradient:
- **P1 (8):** recognisable start, two linked techniques, at least one deduction.
- **P2 (10):** two/three connected methods, no explicitly signposted starting method.
- **P3 (12):** multi-step/unfamiliar reasoning, restrictions, proof or justification.
- **M1 (12):** turn context into model, solve and interpret.
- **M2 (14):** at least three skills, validate/model/evaluate, include limitation.
- **A1 (4):** accessible methods; **A2 (8):** connected application; **A3 (10):** extended statistics/mechanics reasoning.
- **R1–R10 (2 marks each):** short, **independent** retrieval across each headline week's important subskills; at least two spaced retrieval returns from past weeks.

## Detailed weekly authoring briefs

### Week 01 — Algebra, functions and proof
- **Pure:** Indices, surds, quadratics, discriminants, polynomial factors, inequalities, partial fractions, modulus, function composition/inverses, transformations, proof.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Sampling, data types, large data set, mean/median/quartiles/standard deviation, histograms.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Indices/surds; quadratics; discriminants; factor theorem; inequalities; partial fractions; modulus; composites; inverses; transformations.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Prove a quadratic bound via completing the square; disprove a stronger claim; solve a related inequality.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Investigate a restricted transformed function, inverse, domain/range and a composite inequality.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Analyse a rational function using partial fractions, asymptotes and sign intervals.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Use an algebraic ticket-demand and cost model to find profitable prices and evaluate limitations.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Compare two advertising-profit models via composition, algebra and differentiation; justify a choice.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Choose a sampling technique and classify variables.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Calculate and interpret summary statistics and the effect of a transformation.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Interpret a histogram, make an estimate and evaluate sampling bias.

### Week 02 — Coordinate geometry and vectors
- **Pure:** Lines, circles, tangents, intersections, vector magnitudes, position vectors, 2D/3D vectors and geometric proof.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Mechanics modelling/units; SUVAT; vertical motion.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Line equations; gradients; perpendicularity; circle equations; tangents; line-circle intersections; vector addition; magnitude; unit vectors; ratio/position vectors.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Use the discriminant to distinguish tangency and intersection.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Prove collinearity using position vectors and ratios.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Combine circles, vectors and algebra in a geometric proof.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Find feasible routes around circular boundaries.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Investigate parametric geometric designs and optimise under restrictions.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Units and modelling assumptions.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Constant-acceleration journey with sign convention.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Two-stage vertical-motion problem: displacement, distance and assumptions.

### Week 03 — Trigonometry and periodic models
- **Pure:** Exact values, identities/equations, graphs, radians, sine/cosine rule, compound/double angles, R sin(x+a), small-angle approximations.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Year 1 probability; trees and Venn diagrams; independent and mutually exclusive events.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Exact values; radians; sine/cosine rule; trig graphs; identities; compound angles; double angles; reciprocal functions; Rsin form; small angles.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Prove an identity and solve an equation over an interval.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Re-express a trig expression in Rsin form, find an extremum and solve an inequality.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Use the ambiguous sine-rule case and justify all possibilities.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Determine threshold exceedance in a periodic phenomenon.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Analyse a periodic signal using identities, derivatives and approximation limits.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Independent versus mutually exclusive events.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Probability tree for dependent events.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Venn/tree interpretation and independence assumptions.

### Week 04 — Differentiation and curve behaviour
- **Pure:** First principles, chain/product/quotient rules, implicit/parametric differentiation, stationary points, tangents/normals, rates, second derivatives and optimisation.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Force diagrams; resolving forces; Newton's laws.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** First principles; fractional powers; chain; product; quotient; implicit; parametric; exponential/log derivatives; stationary points; tangents.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Investigate stationary points in a parameterised expression.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Use implicit derivatives to find a tangent and its intersections.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Analyse a parametric curve including stationary behaviour.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Optimise a manufacturing/packaging model with restrictions.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Compare two changing-quantity models using first/second derivatives and limitations.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Correct force diagram.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Resolve forces and apply Newton's second law.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Force model followed by a constant-acceleration stage.

### Week 05 — Integration and accumulation
- **Pure (9MA0):** Reverse chain rule; definite/indefinite integrals; substitution; integration by parts (including cyclical cases); linear partial fractions after division; areas under/between curves (including parametric curves); limits of sums.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Binomial distributions and Year 1 hypothesis tests. Conditional probability with its full formula belongs to Year 2, so do not use it as Year 1 core practice.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Power rule; exponential/logarithmic and trigonometric forms; reverse chain; substitution; integration by parts; partial fractions; definite integrals; **area**; limits of sums.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Select an integration method after algebraic division and partial fractions.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Establish the total enclosed area using intersections and sign changes.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Combine repeated integration by parts with a further weighted integral and prove a result.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Interpret an accumulated flow, average rate and limitations.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Calculate the integral of a squared sensor pulse, with parameter sensitivity and restrictions.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Recognise a binomial distribution and justify its parameters.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Calculate cumulative, complementary and strictly bounded binomial probabilities.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Complete a one-tailed binomial hypothesis test and contextual conclusion.
- **T1/T2/T3 (32 marks):** Exact-vs-trapezium area, parametric areas and a two-tailed binomial decision.
- **X1 (14 marks):** A/A* finite accumulated response with repeated integration by parts and a numerical threshold.

**Curriculum correction:** The 9MA0 integration specification (section P8.3) includes definite integrals, planar area between curves and parametric areas, but **does not require volumes of revolution**, which are Further Maths content. Earlier planning references to revolving solids in Week 5 have therefore been replaced with integration and area models that use examinable 9MA0 methods. See the [Pearson enhanced content guidance](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/pearson-edexcel-l3-advanced-gce-mathematics-9ma0-enhanced-content-guidance-issue-2.pdf).

### Week 06 — Sequences, series and binomial expansion
- **Pure:** Arithmetic/geometric sequences and series; sigma; recurrence; infinite geometric sums; binomial integer/non-integer expansion; validity; proof.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Variable acceleration and displacement versus distance.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Arithmetic term; arithmetic sum; geometric term; geometric finite sum; infinite sum; recurrence; sigma; integer binomial; non-integer binomial; validity.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Find when a geometric model crosses a threshold.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Use a non-integer expansion and coefficient comparisons.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Relate recurrence and explicit formula and prove a series result.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Compare discrete and continuous growth.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Evaluate a financial/population cumulative growth model.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Differentiate displacement to obtain v and a.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Integrate v or a with initial conditions.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Changing directions: distance, displacement and modelling.

### Week 07 — Exponentials, logarithms and differential equations
- **Pure:** Log laws and equations; domain; exponential growth/decay; linearisation; separable DEs; initial conditions; model critique.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Regression, interpretation and two-tailed Year 1 binomial tests.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Log laws; exponential equations; log equations; domains; exp derivative; exp integral; linearisation; separation; initial conditions; modelling.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Solve log equation with domain checks.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Determine model parameters via linearisation.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Solve a separable DE with restrictions.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Build exponential model from observations.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Compare two differential-equation models and propose justified refinement.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Interpret regression line/scatter.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Outliers, interpolation and extrapolation.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Two-tailed binomial test with a justified conclusion.

### Week 08 — Numerical methods and synthesis
- **Pure:** Change of sign, fixed-point iteration, Newton-Raphson, trapezium rule, convergence, approximation limits, earlier Pure synthesis.
- **Year 1 Applied:** Connected particles, smooth pulleys, forces and kinematics.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** Change of sign; continuity limits; iteration; cobweb/staircase; Newton-Raphson; derivative; trapezium; bias/error; interpreted roots; calculus recall.
- **P1 (8 marks):** Approximate a root with explanation of starting interval.
- **P2 (10 marks):** Compare trapezium estimate and exact integral and error direction.
- **P3 (12 marks):** Assess convergence/starting point for an iteration.
- **M1 (12 marks):** Optimise a model requiring numerical solution.
- **M2 (14 marks):** Compare analytical and numerical predictions and their reliability.
- **A1 (4 marks):** Connected-particle force diagrams.
- **A2 (8 marks):** Solve acceleration and tension.
- **A3 (10 marks):** Multi-stage connected-particle motion with modelling assumptions.

### Week 09 — Synoptic problem-solving
- **Pure:** All Pure topics; unlabelled methods; extended proofs and integrated problem-solving.
- **Year 1 Applied:** All Year 1 Statistics and Mechanics.
- **AO1 (R1–R10):** 8–10 rapid mixed marks.
- **P1 (8 marks):** 50-min Pure set A (algebra, geometry, trig, series, proof; ~40 marks).
- **P2 (10 marks):** 50-min Pure set B (calculus, exponentials, numerical methods, vectors; ~40 marks).
- **P3 (12 marks):** 45-min Year 1 Applied set (~30–35 marks).
- **M1 (12 marks):** 45-min unstructured modelling challenge (12–16 marks).
- **M2 (14 marks):** 35-min marking and error analysis.
- **Week 9 authored resolution (November 2026):** The earlier P1/P2/P3 descriptions request 30–40-mark timed papers inside 8/10/12-mark question slots. Those two mark/time requirements cannot both be true in the existing fixed 22-question and part-marked interface. Week 9 therefore uses P1/P2/P3 as 8-, 10- and 12-mark integrated Pure problems; Year 1 Applied synthesis is delivered by A1–A3; M1/M2 remain the 12-/14-mark modelling/error-analysis questions. T1–T3 supply a distinct timed 32-mark set. The idea of separate 40-mark papers is retained as a potential future mock-paper format, not falsely advertised as part of these questions.


### Week 10 — Mock examination and consolidation (authored format)
- **Pure:** 12 original questions `W10-P1`–`W10-P12`; **100 marks / 120 minutes**. The full Pure course, including proof, algebra, circle/vector geometry, series, trigonometry, logs/exponentials, implicit/parametric reasoning, integration, numerical methods and 3D vectors, is represented.
- **Year 1 Applied:** 10 original questions `W10-A1`–`W10-A10`; **50 marks / 60 minutes**, split precisely into **25 marks of Year 1 Statistics** (sampling, grouped data, probability, binomial and binomial testing) and **25 marks of Year 1 Mechanics** (straight-line motion, variable acceleration, force models, gravity and connected particles).
- **60-minute review:** dedicated `#/mock-review/10` marking and diagnostic workspace (20 minutes self-marking + 20 minutes identifying the first lost mark + 20 minutes specifying reattempts). This is a *review task*, not a fake 60-mark mathematics question.
- **Exam interface:** Week 10 uses the same calendar and saved question work, but section P is the complete Pure paper and section A the Applied paper. Students may opt into a 120/60-minute timed sitting which hides hints, worked schemes, scores and marking controls while it runs. Each question is self-marked afterwards; absent scores remain unattempted, while a genuine zero counts as marked. Paper totals are shown only once every question has a recorded mark.
- **Total:** 22 original questions, 150 marks over 180 minutes of examination, followed by 60 minutes of review. This is a deliberate *exception* to the 98-mark essential practice-week template used in Weeks 1–9. The optional five- and six-hour practice can target error-based reattempts, rather than fabricate extra mock marks.
- **AO profile:** the full 150-mark paper set has resource-authored approximate AO allocation **AO1 75 / AO2 38 / AO3 37**, close to Pearson's 50% / 25% / 25% qualification-level weighting. This is an authoring target, not a claim of official examination status.

## Required question record and student experience

Every question is an independently addressable item:
- Permanent id e.g. `W04-P2`; programme id; week; section; title; text parts; mark totals and time estimate.
- AO weighting **per part**; topic labels and precise spec codes; difficulty; compulsory/recommended/optional; links to prerequisites and related items.
- Correct answer, **fully worked** step-by-step mark scheme (and alternative solutions where sensible), a restrained *first-step hint*, pitfalls/common misconceptions, and a reflection prompt.
- For mathematical text use a proper formula renderer; keyboard-friendly controls, accessible expandable hint/solution regions, responsive layouts.
- Student attempt status: **I** = Independent, **P** = Prompt needed, **S** = Still stuck. Separate record for errors: technical skill / method selection / reasoning / interpretation / calculator / time.
- Self-mark does not auto-assign mathematical accuracy without evidence; students manually record marks after comparing to worked solution.
- The site must never claim a scheduled but unpublished set is ready or count blueprint placeholders as finished questions.
- Future questions must not be shipped in the public front-end bundle while locked if the intent is **genuine content embargo**. A public static Pages site can only offer navigational locks and a manual build/publish workflow, not secret future question protection.

## Quality gates (every item)

1. Genuine mathematical decision; not merely a named technique.
2. Topic connection is necessary, not pasted-on.
3. AO attributions reflect steps and marks, not surface-level context.
4. Numbers, ranges, units, roots and mark scheme independently checked.
5. Workload tried or calibrated to time budget.
